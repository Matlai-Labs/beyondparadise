## When to go

Ngorongoro's dry season, June through October, is the standard recommendation for wildlife viewing, with July and August marking peak tourism months. A second strong window falls in January and February, driven by the wildebeest calving season happening nearby in the southern Serengeti — animals concentrate and predator activity follows, similar to the dynamic covered in our [Great Migration guide](/wildlife/great-migration-serengeti-masai-mara/).

The wet season (November–May) is a genuine trade-off rather than simply "worse": short rains run November–December and long rains March–May, but this window brings lusher scenery, excellent birdwatching from November through April, meaningfully fewer tourists, and sometimes lower accommodation rates during the long rains specifically.

## A concrete planning note: the crater-rim lodge situation

andBeyond Ngorongoro Crater Lodge — the best-known property directly on the crater rim — ceased operations in January 2025 for a full rebuild and isn't expected to reopen until 2027. Before its closure, it comprised 30 stilted suites split across three camps (North, South, and Tree Camp) with a 60-guest total capacity, at all-inclusive rates that ran roughly $1,160–$1,500+ per night depending on suite type, covering meals, house wines and spirits, game-drive refreshments, laundry, and emergency medical evacuation insurance.

We're flagging this specifically because plenty of older travel content still references this lodge without noting the closure — if you're planning a Ngorongoro trip built around a crater-rim stay, confirm current lodge availability directly rather than assuming this property is bookable.

## Conservation context — worth asking about, not yet independently verified

andBeyond has stated it partnered with the Ngorongoro Conservation Area Authority (NCAA) and Tanzania's Ministry of Natural Resources and Tourism on a southern white rhino translocation from South Africa, and separately runs a KopeLion guest-engagement programme with a per-interaction donation model. These claims currently trace to the operator itself rather than an independent source — we're including them because they're the kind of specific, checkable commitment worth asking your lodge to substantiate directly, not because we've independently confirmed them.

## Entry requirements and health

Tanzania's standard visa rules apply: EU, US, and UK travelers need a visa, with e-visa application recommended well in advance. US citizens specifically require a $100 multiple-entry visa. A yellow fever certificate is required if arriving from or transiting more than 12 hours through a risk country (generally not an issue for direct travel from Europe or the US).

One genuinely useful detail: the crater rim itself, sitting above 1,800 meters, carries only minimal malaria risk — a meaningful contrast to most of Tanzania's lower-elevation safari zones, where prophylaxis is strongly recommended.

## Getting there

Kilimanjaro International Airport (JRO), between Moshi and Arusha, is the primary gateway for the whole northern safari circuit, with direct or seasonal European connections (KLM, Brussels Airlines, Air France, Edelweiss Air, Neos, Turkish Airlines) plus links via Nairobi or Addis Ababa. From JRO, you transfer to Arusha — the actual gateway town for this circuit — then continue by road roughly 160–190 km (3–4 hours) to the Ngorongoro area. Most itineraries combine this leg with the [Serengeti](/east-africa/serengeti/) or Tarangire rather than treating Ngorongoro as a standalone trip.

## What this means for planning your trip

Target June–October or January–February for wildlife-focused timing, but don't build your accommodation plan around the crater-rim lodge that's currently closed — confirm what's actually bookable before you commit dates. If birding or a quieter, lower-cost trip appeals more than peak-season wildlife density, the wet season is a legitimate alternative most itineraries overlook by default.
