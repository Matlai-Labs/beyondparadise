## Two dry seasons, one to avoid

Zanzibar's climate splits into three windows, and knowing which one you're booking into changes what the trip actually delivers:

| Season | Months | What it's good for |
|---|---|---|
| Long dry (Kaskazi) | June–October | The primary window — clear skies, 26–30°C, low humidity, best all-round conditions; coincides with mainland Tanzania's migration season |
| Short dry | December–February | Secondary good window — hot, sunny, strong diving and snorkelling conditions, especially the south coast in Feb–March |
| Long rains (Masika) | March–May | Actively avoid for a beach-first trip — heavy rainfall, rougher seas, reduced marine visibility, though diving visibility can occasionally be excellent as winds subside |

If your itinerary is flexible, June–October is the safer default; if you're specifically chasing marine wildlife, check the relevant species guide, since whale sharks, humpbacks, and turtle nesting each run their own separate calendar (see the [migration calendar](/intelligence/migration-calendar/) for the full cross-species picture).

## The travel insurance requirement most guides miss

Since October 1, 2024, every non-resident visitor to Zanzibar must carry mandatory Inbound Travel Insurance from the Zanzibar Insurance Corporation (ZIC), covering stays up to 92 days at a cost of USD 44. This is a genuinely recent rule change, and plenty of older travel content simply doesn't mention it. Other international travel insurance policies — even comprehensive ones — are not accepted as a substitute. If you already have travel insurance from home, budget for the ZIC policy as an additional, non-optional cost, not a redundant one.

## Diving and kitesurfing: two different calendars

Diving is close to a year-round activity given consistently warm water (25–29°C), but visibility and sea state shift by month — September through March offers the calmest seas and the best visibility, reaching up to 30 meters at its peak.

Kitesurfing runs on an entirely separate schedule from diving, centered on Paje Beach: Kaskazi season (mid-December to February/March) brings north winds averaging 15–18 knots, while Kuzi season (roughly May to September/October) brings generally stronger south-to-southeast winds averaging 15–25 knots. March, April, May, October, and November are the off-season for kiting specifically — note this only partially overlaps with the "avoid" window for general beach travel (March–May), since Kuzi season extends the kiting-good months later into the year than the general dry-season calendar suggests.

## Entry requirements and health

Zanzibar is part of Tanzania, so Tanzania's visa system applies. EU and UK passport holders can typically get a single-entry e-visa or visa on arrival for around $50, valid 90 days. US citizens must apply for a $100 multiple-entry visa valid for 12 months — there's no single-entry option, and the U.S. Embassy strongly recommends securing it in advance rather than on arrival.

A yellow fever certificate is mandatory if you're arriving from or transiting more than 12 hours through a risk country. Malaria is present across Zanzibar, and — importantly — chloroquine is not an effective prophylaxis here due to drug resistance; atovaquone-proguanil, doxycycline, mefloquine, or tafenoquine are the recommended alternatives. Hepatitis A/B, typhoid, and routine vaccinations are commonly recommended for Tanzania travel more broadly.

## Getting there and around

There are no direct flights from the UK or most of continental Europe. Expect a connection through a Middle East hub (Doha, Dubai, Istanbul) or an East African hub (Nairobi, Addis Ababa, Dar es Salaam) — though some European carriers (Condor, Turkish Airlines, KLM, Air France, Edelweiss) run direct or seasonal routes depending on your departure city. From Abeid Amani Karume International Airport (ZNZ), Stone Town is a short 8–15 minute taxi ride costing $5–15; the east coast beach areas (Paje, Jambiani) run roughly 1–1.5 hours by road.

## What this means for planning your trip

Book around June–October by default unless you have a specific reason not to, budget for the ZIC insurance policy as a real line item rather than an afterthought, and check the kitesurfing and diving calendars separately if either is a priority — they don't move in lockstep with each other or with the general "best season" advice.
