Mnemba Atoll Snorkeling from Kendwa or Stone Town

By Tim Hennig  ·  Updated 2026-11-15

The Short Answer

From Kendwa, one operator listing records a beach pickup around 8:50 am, a boat ride of about 45 minutes and a 9:30 am to 3:30 pm day. From Stone Town or the airport the published access route is a 45-90 minute road transfer to Matemwe, then a 10-20 minute boat. Non-East African visitors pay a $10 adult, $5 child conservation fee (rates reported from 1 September 2025).

Two ways to get there

Mnemba Atoll sits off Zanzibar’s northeast coast, reached via the Matemwe coast. The Kendwa version is a boat day: pickup at the beach, about 45 minutes on the water, and, in the one operator listing we hold (The Abroad Guide, checked 23 September 2026), a day that also included dolphin snorkelling and a seafood lunch. Other operators will package it differently. The Stone Town version is a land-and-sea transfer, and guests wade ashore to the boat because there are no jetties. We record the Kendwa timing from a single operator listing and the Stone Town route from the access description of a lodge on the atoll, so treat both as estimates and ask your operator for exact times.

Kendwa also suits boat departures for a reason that is recorded: its beach stays swimmable through the tide cycle thanks to a deep shelf close offshore, unlike neighbouring Nungwi, where the water can recede a long way at low tide (Altezza Travel and Destinationz, checked 23 September 2026).

When to go

The two sources we use name June to October and December to February or March as the best snorkelling windows, with calm seas and visibility of 20-30 metres. Our Zanzibar seasons data lists October to March as the calmest marine window overall. The two lists overlap but are not identical, so a December to February trip falls in both. See the migration and marine wildlife calendar for the wider picture.

What the reef holds

Roughly 600 species of reef fish and 36 hard coral genera are recorded in the Mnemba Island Marine Conservation Area. As of January 2024, the Oceans Without Borders team had transplanted 7,193 coral colonies from its nursery onto degraded parts of the house reef (Wild Impact and &Beyond, checked 30 June 2026). Green sea turtles, an Endangered species, breed here; read sea turtles in Zanzibar for nesting months, which we do not tie to a single Mnemba peak.

Fees and access rules

Non-East African visitors pay $10 per adult and $5 per child aged 5-15 for the Mnemba Island Conservation Area, rates reported from 1 September 2025 by two tour operators; a “Mnemba Special Area” is reported at $10 to $25. Mnemba Island is privately owned by &Beyond. Non-guests cannot land, and a 500-metre exclusion zone applies. Confirm the fee and whether your operator includes it.

Snorkel without harming it

The ethics guidance recorded for this atoll is plain: do not touch marine life or coral, do not stand on coral, use reef-safe sunscreen, and do not support operators who feed marine life or chase dolphins. Concerns about unethical dolphin tours and crowding at popular snorkel spots, including Mnemba, are also on record. Our Menai Bay dolphin guide sets out what responsible operator selection looks like.

What we do not know yet

We hold no price, boat type, group size or operator name for either route, and no first-hand report of a Mnemba day. Beyond Paradise Adventures does not sell this trip. Your lodge can confirm the current operator and cost.

Frequently Asked Questions

How long is the boat ride to Mnemba from Kendwa?

About 45 minutes, in a day recorded as running 9:30 am to 3:30 pm after a beach pickup around 8:50 am (The Abroad Guide listing, checked 23 September 2026). This is one operator's schedule, not a standard, so confirm times when you book.

How do I reach Mnemba from Stone Town or the airport?

By road to the Matemwe coast, 45-90 minutes, then a 10-20 minute boat, with a wade through shallow water at the end because there is no jetty (the access route published for &Beyond Mnemba Island Lodge, checked 28 June 2026). Day-trip boats may differ.

When is the best time to snorkel Mnemba?

The sources we use name the dry seasons: June to October and December to February or March, with calm seas and underwater visibility of 20-30 metres (checked 30 June 2026). Visibility varies day to day.

Is there a fee, and can I land on the island?

Non-East African visitors are charged $10 per adult and $5 per child aged 5-15 for the Mnemba Island Conservation Area, rates reported from 1 September 2025 by two tour operators (checked 30 June 2026); confirm when booking. The island is privately owned and non-guests cannot land, with a 500-metre exclusion zone around it.

Will I see dolphins and turtles?

Dolphins and green turtles are reported often, but no sighting is guaranteed. Green turtles are listed Endangered on the IUCN Red List. Choose an operator that does not chase or feed animals; our guide to Menai Bay dolphins explains why this matters.
